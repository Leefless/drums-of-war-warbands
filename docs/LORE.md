# Drums of War: Warbands

## Lore of the Broken Marches

Before the Merging, the world had edges. A mountain was a mountain, a wood was a wood, and the dead stayed where they were put. Then two realms were forced into one, and the seams have been bleeding ever since.

This is the account of what broke, who is left, and why six armies now fight their wars in handfuls instead of in hosts.

---

## The Merging

### Before

The old world was a patchwork of kingdoms that mostly kept to themselves. The Dwarves had carved their holds deep into the mountains long before anyone thought to count years. High Elves kept their libraries and their arcane studies, Wood Elves kept the ancient woods, and the Orks, Goblins and Trolls kept to the green places between, raiding when hungry and feuding when bored. Humans and Halflings farmed, traded and built walls. The Giant Kin were explorers then, wandering the far ranges and mapping what the other peoples never saw.

It was not peace, but it was a world with rules. Wars were fought by great hosts on open ground, with banners, heralds and a day set aside for the killing.

### The Calamity

Nobody agrees on what triggered the Merging, only on what it did. The Calamity Realm, a place with no fixed shape and no mercy, was pressed into the world like a thumb into wet clay. Where the realms met, the land cracked. Those cracks are the Rifts, and through them came the Calamity itself: raw, hungry power that does not distinguish between a weapon and a wound.

The consequences were immediate and lasting:

- Creatures near the Rifts changed. Some became Calamity-touched beasts. Some people became Mutants, and were hunted for it.
- Demons of Calamity walked out of the seams in the shapes of whatever the watchers feared most.
- Healing magic faltered. Magic flows from the soul, and the Calamity fights back against anything that mends.
- Giant Kin turned from exploring to conquering, and Kobold terror curdled into hatred.
- The Deep Elves turned to darkness, and the Umbralis rose: undead of concentrated shadow and will, able to pass for the living and eager to gather the forsaken dead.
- The Lumanari were born, children of light that came out of the Calamity itself, yet they sense it and oppose it.

### The Present Age

The old great hosts are gone, and the reason is simple. The Calamity answers mass. A host of thousands, with its drums and its fear and its single crowded purpose, draws Rifts the way a lightning rod draws a storm. Ground that holds ten thousand soldiers for a week tears open on the third night. Whole armies have been swallowed on the march, and what came back out of the Rift was not them.

So the Broken Marches are fought over by warbands: a commander, a few units, and a handful of specialists, small enough to move fast and strike hard before the land notices. Every army has learned the same lesson in a different way. Keep the force small. Keep it moving. End the fight before the ground does.

The Marches are the shattered country between the old kingdoms, and everyone wants a piece of them. Three kinds of ground decide the fighting there:

- **Ruins.** Fallen watchtowers, collapsed holds and hollowed-out chapels from the world before. They give cover and block the line, and rumor says some of them still remember their builders.
- **Thickets.** Tangled growth that has spread unchecked since the Merging. Most armies must hack or wade through it. The Sylvan Courts do not.
- **Rifts.** Open seams where the Calamity Realm shows through. Stepping near one is a risk. Using one is power, but never a free one.

## The Iron Holds

*Dwarves, with Giant Kin sellswords.*

**Who they are.** The Dwarves carved their kingdoms in the mountains before the Merging, and they remember that. The Holds are old, proud and bitter, and every Hold-Thane carries the weight of a line that stretches back to the unbroken world.

**What they want.** Their ground. Not conquest, but the return of every stone ever taken from them, and the right to hold it in peace.

**How they fight.** Slowly, with the full weight of oath behind each step. Their top speed is 3, and every turn is a strain, because Dwarves do not turn: they face forward and advance. But they stop cleanly and they are Unyielding: a unit that halts or steps straight ahead may take a second action, to steel itself or brace. The Calamity cannot move a Dwarf who has decided not to be moved, and Brace tokens turn aside a blow after the dice have already fallen.

- **Hold-Thane**: Oath-bound lord of a mountain hold. He leads from the front line and rarely retreats.
- **Ironbreaker Shieldline**: Locked shields, rune-etched mail. They advance one step at a time and do not step back.
- **Thunderbarge Gunners**: Blackpowder and grudges. Useless at close quarters, devastating from range.
- **Runepriest**: Carves wards into stone and steel. The runes knit closed as fast as the enemy breaks them.
- **Grudgebreaker Cannon**: Each shot has a name chiselled into the ball. It reaches across the whole field.
- **Giant-Kin Hurlers**: Giant Kin who sell their strength to the Holds for ale and a share of the hoard.

**Rival.** The Greenskin Warhorde. Orks have raided Dwarf halls for as long as there have been halls. The Dwarves keep a ledger of every offense, and the ledger is long.

## The Sylvan Courts

*Wood Elves and High Elves.*

**Who they are.** An uneasy alliance of the Wood Elves who protect the ancient woods and the High Elf scholars who study arcane power. They share a long memory and a belief that the Merging was a wound to be healed, not a war to be fought head on.

**What they want.** To close the Rifts without breaking the woods, and to keep what remains of the old world from being burned for fuel.

**How they fight.** Quickly, and never where you aimed. Their dials run from speed 1 to 4, every turn is white, and the gentle banks are blue. Fleetfoot means most actions link into a red Sidestep or Surge: loose an arrow, step aside, loose again. Wildwood means the Thickets that stall everyone else do not slow them at all. The cost is fragility. A Sylvan unit lives on defence dice and distance, and a unit pinned in the open is a unit lost.

- **Lady of the Glade**: A thousand years old and still the quickest blade in the wood.
- **Glade Wardens**: Wood Elf hunters. Loose, step aside, and loose again.
- **Thornriders**: Riders on great antlered elk that run through the trees without breaking stride.
- **Starweaver**: A High Elf scholar-mage whose wards are woven fresh with every breath.
- **Bladesinger Court**: High Elf duellists who fight as a single dance, almost impossible to land a blow on.

**Rival.** The Umbral Covenant. The Deep Elves were their kin once, and the Umbralis wear the faces of the Courts' own dead.

## The Greenskin Warhorde

*Orks, Goblins, and Trolls.*

**Who they are.** The Green Skin tongue binds Orks, Goblins and Trolls into one roaring mass. They did not need the Merging to start fighting, but the Merging gave them more to fight about.

**What they want.** More: more land, more loot, more enemies, and a louder noise than the last Warboss made.

**How they fight.** Fast and loud, with no stop on the dial and few slow moves. Mob Rule means a Greenskin is only Strained at 2 strain, so the horde chains hard red maneuvers that would break a Dwarf. Momentum rewards the charge: any maneuver at speed 4 or more grants a Frenzy token, and a frenzied attacker rerolls its dice. They have no Guard action, because they do not dodge. They do not need to. The Calamity prefers to draw on mass, but Greenskin warbands outrun it, and when a Rift opens behind them, they are already gone.

- **Warboss**: Biggest, meanest, loudest. The horde follows the noise he makes.
- **Goblin Rabble**: Individually pathetic. Collectively, a problem.
- **Ork Boyz**: They do not dodge. They do not need to.
- **Boar Riders**: War-boars with iron tusks, ridden by Orks who think brakes are for Goblins.
- **Troll Smashas**: Cut one down and it gets back up, angrier.
- **Weirdboy Shaman**: Soaks up the horde's noise and spits it back out as lightning.

**Rival.** The Iron Holds. The oldest feud in the Marches, and neither side has any plan to end it.

## The Umbral Covenant

*Umbralis masters and their risen dead.*

**Who they are.** The Umbralis are undead of concentrated shadow and will. They pass for the living, wear borrowed faces, and gather the forsaken dead of every battlefield, because the Marches have no shortage of those. Alongside them stand the Necromancers, who harvest the dying and refuse to let anyone rest.

**What they want.** An empire of the dead, ordered and tireless, where nothing is ever wasted and nothing ever stops.

**How they fight.** By strings. The Skeleton Levy, Ghoul Pack and Bone Colossus have no actions of their own: they march and strike on their own, but they take actions only when a master spends one to Command them, and each master takes two actions per activation. Take the masters, and the thralls simply stand. The dead are also Deathless: they never gain strain, and whatever would strain them wounds them instead, because the undead have no rest to lose. Their dials run speed 1 to 3 with every turn white and no blue banks. The dead cannot ease into a turn, they only make it.

- **Umbralis Shade**: Concentrated shadow and will, wearing a borrowed face.
- **Necromancer**: Harvests the dying. Every death nearby is fuel to mend the risen.
- **Skeleton Levy**: Yesterday's dead, today's front line.
- **Ghoul Pack**: Fast, starving, and barely leashed.
- **Wraith Host**: They pass through walls and shields alike. The living break near them.
- **Bone Colossus**: A cathedral of bones that walks.

**Rival.** The Dawnward Concord. The Witch Hunters burn what the Covenant raises, and the Lumanari see the shadow-and-will behind every borrowed face.

## The Calamity Host

*Demons of Calamity, Calamity Mages, and the Mutant-touched.*

**Who they are.** The children of the Rift: Demons that crossed over, Mages who took the taint willingly for power beyond mortal limits, and Mutants who were hunted until they stopped running. Kahr is their language, and it is not a tongue made for speech.

**What they want.** Different things, held together by a shared understanding that the Marches belong to whatever the Rifts say they do. The Demons want more of the world. The Mages want more power. The Mutants want to stop being hunted.

**How they fight.** With power that bites back. Overreach lets every unit take a second action, but then it rolls a Taint die, and a Strike costs 1 damage while a Rend is a critical wound. Channel banks Calamity charges, up to 2, that turn any die into a Rend. The Rifts themselves feed the Host: where other armies are burned by an open seam, a Calamity unit drinks a charge from it. Rift maneuvers teleport a unit straight ahead, ignoring everything in the way. Plain and white at low speed, red at the Rift jumps of 2 to 4. They are mighty and they are mortal, and the Calamity keeps the ledger.

- **Demon of Calamity**: A thing from the Calamity Realm, wearing the shape of every nightmare at once.
- **Spawn of Calamity**: Unstable flesh that bursts when it dies.
- **Tainted Mutants**: Hunted by Witch Hunters for what they became. They have stopped running.
- **Calamity Mage**: Took the taint willingly. Power far beyond mortal limits, for now.
- **Rift Stalker**: Steps through the seams of the world and out behind you.

**Rival.** The Dawnward Concord. Every Witch Hunter cadre exists to end the Host, and the Host has more reasons than any other army to want the Hunters dead.

## The Dawnward Concord

*Humans, Halflings, and Lumanari, led by the Witch Hunters.*

**Who they are.** A coalition of farmers, scholars, sellswords, Halflings and Lumanari under the banner of the Witch Hunters, who have spent years hunting Calamity cultists. Individually they are the weakest people in the Marches. Together they are the only army that does not need to be strong, because they do not fight alone.

**What they want.** The dawn: a day the Rifts stop, the Merging is undone or survived, and the Marches are safe to farm again.

**How they fight.** As a combined arm. Their dials are balanced at speeds 1 to 4, with blue gentle banks and red hard turns at speed 3. Rally lets a Concord unit remove strain even while Strained, because discipline is what holds a line when it should break. Commanders and Banners let one unit spend its action to give another a free one, so no unit is ever really acting alone.

- **Knight-Captain**: Holds a line of farmers, scholars, and halflings together by voice alone.
- **Free Company Halberdiers**: Paid in coin, kept in line by drill.
- **Crossbow Line**: Bolts that punch through mail and demon hide alike.
- **Halfling Outriders**: Easy to miss, quick of hand, and cheerfully lethal with a stone.
- **Witch Hunter Cadre**: Grim hunters of Calamity corruption. Silvered steel and no mercy.
- **Lumanari Lightbearer**: Born of light, they sense the Calamity-touched and burn them.

**Rival.** The Calamity Host. The Concord was founded to oppose it, and the Silent Order wardens who stand with them know exactly what happens to minds the Calamity touches.

## Why the Drums

Before the Merging, a host marched to the beat of a drum, and the drum told every soldier where to be. Now the drums are quiet, because the drumbeat of ten thousand feet is the sound that opens Rifts. The Broken Marches have learned to fight in silence, and each army has learned to fight in its own way.

But the drums have not gone away. They have moved inside. A Dwarf hears them in each step of the Shieldline. An Elf hears them in the pulse of a bowstring. An Ork hears them in the Warboss's roar, the dead hear them in the strings that pull them, and the Concord hears them in the Captain's voice. Even the Calamity beats a drum, and it is the loudest of all.

Every warband is a small drum. Every battle is a question of whose rhythm holds, and whose breaks, when the ground starts to tear.

The drums of war are still beating. You choose who walks to them.
